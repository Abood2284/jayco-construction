## The Paradigm Shift in Ergonomic Loading

Traditional scissor lift tables often sit **above** the slab when fully lowered, so moving a pallet on with a **manual pallet jack** usually means a **ramp** or a **pit** so the deck meets the floor plane.

**Zero lifting platforms** (zero-level or **low-profile** scissors) are built to collapse to **near floor height**—often on the order of **tens of millimetres**—so **pallet jacks and carts can roll on directly** on a **flat, level floor**, without a pit or long ramp. That saves space and speeds repetitive loading in warehouses and production areas.

## Low-Profile Structural Architecture

* **Roll-on deck geometry:** Many units use a **U-shaped** or **pan** deck so forks enter from the side and the load sits on the platform without climbing an incline.
* **Ultra-low collapsed height:** Achieving **~10–100 mm** class collapsed heights often drives **electro-hydraulic** layouts with the **power pack** beside or away from the table envelope—confirm clearances on the supplier GA.
* **Floor space:** Removing permanent ramps reclaims aisle length in tight layouts.

## Typical Operating Ranges (Industry Guide)

Catalogues vary by region and OEM; treat the following as **early sizing only**:

* **Capacity:** Mainstream lines are often quoted around **~500 kg up to ~5 t**; lighter **~250 kg** builds and **heavy-duty** lines may reach toward **~50,000 lb (~22.5 t)** in specialized tables—**always** use the **rated load** on the **nameplate**.
* **Stroke:** Raised heights are frequently listed around **~800–2,000 mm**, with some models toward **~96 in (~2,400 mm)** or more.
* **Platform:** **Custom** sizes are common; **~1,270 × 1,100 mm** through **~2,400 × 2,100 mm** appears often in marketing sheets.
* **Power:** **380/415 V** three-phase or **220–240 V** supplies with **~1.5–3 kW** class motor power are typical for electro-hydraulic packs—match **local supply** and **code**.

## Hydraulic Mechanics and Safety

* **Motion:** Hydraulic pressure extends the scissors to lift smoothly—useful for **assembly**, **packaging**, and **pallet build/break** at a **fixed ergonomic height**.
* **Safety:** Common equipment includes **velocity fuses** (uncontrolled descent protection), **toe protection** / trip logic, **overload** protection, and **maintenance locks**—exact package depends on the manufacturer and applicable standards.

## Applications

* **Vehicle loading and unloading** at bays where a low profile helps bridge floor to truck sill (within rated use).
* **Factory lines** feeding machines or conveyors at a controlled height.
* **Ergonomic workstations** to limit bending and reaching.

## Practical Selection Guidelines

* **Deck vs load:** Match **platform length/width**, **open side**, and **capacity** to pallet, skid, or fixture size—not only weight.
* **Pallet jack compatibility:** Check **entry width** and **wheel path** against your jack model; **Euro** vs **wide** pallets differ.
* **Floor:** **Level, smooth** slabs help the **zero-height** concept work; crown, joints, or debris can block **roll-on**.
* **Automation:** **Interlocks**, **signals**, and **one-touch** sequences are optional—define with your integrator and the OEM manual.
