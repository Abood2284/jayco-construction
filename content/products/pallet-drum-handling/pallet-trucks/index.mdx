## The Foundational Unit of Material Handling

**Pallet trucks** (pallet jacks, pump trucks) lift and move **palletized** loads without a counterbalance forklift’s footprint. The load is carried on **forks** and **load rollers**, so the truck stays **low** and **short** for **dock** and **aisle** work—provided **fork length**, **overall width**, and **wheel** choice match **pallet** and **floor**.

## Typical Specifications (Industry Catalogue Bands)

Mainstream **manual** hydraulic jacks are often sold in roughly **~2,500 kg (2.5 t)** class for “standard” warehouse duty; lighter **~1,000 kg** and heavier **~3,000 kg** lines are common, with **~5,000 kg** **specialized** heavy-duty designs. **Fork length** is frequently **~1,150 mm** for **Euro** (800 × 1,200 mm) compatibility and **~1,220 mm** for many **48 in** stringer pallets—always check **pallet** and **opening** dimensions.

**Height** (typical bands): **lowered** fork height often **~75–90 mm** (many catalogues center on **~85 mm**); **raised** height after **full pump** often **~190–210 mm** (often quoted around **~200 mm**). **Overall fork width** (outer spread on adjustable models) varies by series—**narrow / Euro**-oriented vs **wide** layouts for **1,000 mm** or **1,200 mm** deck pallets—use the supplier’s **adjustment table**.

**Wheels:** **Polyurethane (PU)** on **steering** and **load** wheels is standard where **noise** and **floor marking** matter; **nylon** suits **rough** or **abrasive** floors. **Tandem** load rollers improve **uneven** slabs or **dock plates** vs single rollers.

**Hydraulics:** **Sealed** pumps with **overload** protection and **controlled lowering** are common; quality lines often use **one-piece** pump bodies and **service** parts from the OEM.

## Manual Pallet Trucks: Mechanics and Ergonomics

* **Lift:** Pump strokes pressurize a **ram** that lifts the fork linkage; **release** valves or **slow-lowering** circuits control descent.
* **Travel:** The operator **pulls** or **pushes**—**manual** trucks are best for **short** runs and **level** floors; **long** distances or **slopes** favor **electric** walkies.

## Electric Pallet Trucks: Throughput and Automation

**Walkies** add **motorized** travel (and often **powered** lift) with **batteries** (lead-acid or lithium-ion classes). They reduce **push/pull** injury risk and raise **throughput** on long **DC** aisles. Expect **braking**, **speed** limits, and **belly** / **reverse** controls on many designs—per OEM manual.

## Alternative Types (Guide)

* **Scissor-lift / high-lift pallet truck:** Raises the deck to **ergonomic** height for **bench** or **conveyor**—capacities are often **lower** than a standard jack (e.g. **up to ~1,000 kg** class in many catalogues; **confirm** per model).
* **Long-fork truck:** **Two pallets** side-by-side or **long** unit loads—forks **~2,000 mm** or **longer** are common in marketing sheets.
* **Stainless / galvanized:** For **food**, **pharma**, or **washdown** / **corrosive** environments.

## Practical Selection Guidelines

* **Pallet:** Match **fork length**, **width**, and **spread** to **ISO** / **Euro** / **US** pallets used on site.
* **Floor:** **Expansion joints**, **grates**, and **slopes**—wheel **diameter** and **roller** layout (tandem vs single) affect **roll** and **noise**.
* **Duty:** **Spare** manual for **backup**; **electric** when **mileage** and **shift length** justify **battery** and **maintenance** cost.
