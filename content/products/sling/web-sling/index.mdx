## The Role of Synthetic Web Slings

**Steel wire** and **chain** are unforgiving on **finished** surfaces. **Web slings** (flat woven belts, often **eye-and-eye**) and **endless-type** synthetic slings spread load over a **wide**, **soft** contact patch, cutting **scratch** and **dent** risk for **machined**, **coated**, or **fragile** loads.

## Material Science: Nylon vs. Polyester

* **Nylon:** Higher **elasticity**—on the order of **~8–10%** stretch at **WLL** in many references—helps **cushion** **shock** in **dynamic** lifts. **Weak** where **acids** or **bleach** attack the fibre; **water** can affect some properties—follow **OEM** limits.
* **Polyester:** **Lower stretch**—often **~3%** at **WLL**—for **tighter** **headroom** control. Generally more **acid**-tolerant than nylon; **strong alkalis** can attack **polyester**—match **chemical** exposure to **MSDS** and **sling** **maker**.

## Construction and Standards

Flat web slings are commonly built in **simplex** (single-ply), **duplex**, or **triplex** weaves for higher **capacity** in the same width. **Eyes** may be **flat**, **reversed**, or **twisted** depending on hardware and **basket** geometry.

**Endless roundslings** (continuous **jacketed** core) share the **synthetic** category: the **wear** **point** can be **rotated** between lifts to extend life.

Manufacture and use are covered by standards such as **EN 1492-1**, **ASME B30.9**, and **IS 15041:2001** in different markets. **Design factors** (minimum **breaking** vs **tagged WLL**) are commonly in the **5:1–7:1** range depending on **standard** and **product** line—see **certificate**.

## Capacity, Color, and Identification

**WLL** tables for **straight** lift often fall in a **~1–12 t** marketing band for mainstream **web** lines; **specialized** **heavy** constructions appear in **hundreds of tonnes** in **some** **supplier** **charts**—treat any **online** **table** as **non-binding** until it matches **your** **tag**.

**Color** stripes are a **convenience** only—**violet/green/yellow/grey/red/brown/blue/orange** schemes appear in **European-style** marketing (e.g. **1–12 t** mapping in many brochures). **Manufacturers** **do** **not** **all** use the **same** **map**—**always** obey the **sewn-in** **metal** or **poly** **tag**: **WLL**, **length**, **material**, **standard**, **traceability**.

**Temperature:** **Polyester** **web** is often quoted about **−40 °C** to **+100 °C** for **ambient** use; **derate** or **change** **sling** **type** for **hot** **lifts** per **OEM**.

## Regulatory Practice and Edge Vulnerability

**WSTDA**-style guidance and **ASME** volumes align with **inspection**, **storage**, and **removal** criteria. The dominant **failure** mode in service is **cut** and **abrasion**:

* **Edge protection:** **Never** **load** **bare** **web** over **sharp** **edges**—use **rated** **guards**, **magnetic** **pads**, or **doubled** **sleeves**.
* **Tag:** If the **tag** is **missing** or **unreadable**, **remove** the **sling** from **overhead** service.
