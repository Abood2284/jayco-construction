## The Complexity of Cylindrical Material Handling

**210 L (55 US gal)** steel and plastic drums are awkward for **pallet forks**: smooth walls, high center of gravity, and often **hazardous** contents. **Drum stackers** and **drum trolleys** combine a **lift** (manual hydraulic or **semi-electric**), a **grip** matched to the **rim**, and sometimes **tilt** or **rotate** for pour or mix—so one operator can **move and stage** drums without rolling them on the floor.

Industry catalogues often quote **~300–500 kg** for mainstream **210 L** stackers; **stroke** may run from **~250 mm** (low **transport** or **palletizer** roles) through **~1,600 mm** typical **stacking**, with some masts toward **~3,000 mm+**. Always use the **nameplate SWL** and **drum type** approved by the manufacturer.

## Gripping Mechanics and Structural Design

Flat forks are replaced by **drum-specific** attachments:

* **Parrot-beak / eagle-grip:** Jaws clamp the **upper chime**; load weight can increase bite force on steel rims—confirm **plastic** or **fiber** drum approval so the rim is not crushed or slipped.
* **Belt / cradle:** Straps or curved supports around the **barrel** suit some plastic drums or odd rims.
* **Lift column:** Often a **simplex** mast in **heavy C-channel**; lift via **foot pump**, **hand lever**, or **battery** hydraulic pump depending on model.

## Precision Rotation and Tilt Functions

Decanting or mixing needs **stable angle**:

* **Gear or worm drives** (hand wheel or powered) give **~180°–360°** rotation bands in supplier literature, with **holding torque** so the drum does not run away mid-pour.
* **Worm-type** layouts can be **self-locking** at the commanded angle—still follow the OEM for **load**, **drum**, and **speed** limits.

## Common Model Families (Guide)

Catalogue naming varies; typical **patterns** include:

* **Manual drum stacker** (e.g. **~350 kg** / **~1.6 m** class): four-wheel base, **360°** rotation on some lines, manual hydraulics.
* **Semi-electric:** **Battery**-powered lift, **manual** push/pull travel—common around **~350 kg**, **~1.6 m** lift in marketing sheets.
* **Three-wheel drum lifter trolley:** **~300–350 kg**, **~1.0–1.5 m** lift—compact for **tight aisles**.
* **Drum palletizer / low stacker:** **~250–300 kg**, **~250–500 mm** lift—minimal stroke for **dock** or **racking** feeding.
* **Stainless steel:** **304/316**-class builds for **food**, **pharma**, or **washdown** when corrosion or contamination must be controlled.

## Safety and Options

* **Positive gripping** and **overload** discipline—never exceed **SWL** or use unapproved drum types.
* **Emergency stop** on powered models; **brakes** on casters for slopes and docks.
* **Optional scales** for weighed decant or inventory.
* **Adjustable width** legs or grippers where multiple drum sizes appear on one site.

## Practical Selection Guidelines

* **Drum list:** Steel vs plastic **rim** geometry, **open** vs **tight** head—gripper must match **every** SKU you run.
* **Height:** Match **rack**, **mezzanine**, or **truck** deck targets; confirm **lowered** height for **door** and **elevator** limits.
* **Zone rating:** **Flammable** stores may exclude standard electrics—use **manual** or **Ex-rated** equipment per assessment.
