## Bridging the Supply Chain Gap

The loading dock is where the warehouse meets the road. Trailer bed heights vary, suspension moves under load, and a fixed concrete step would leave a **gap** and a **height mismatch**. **Dock levelers** are steel platforms installed in a **pit** at the bay: they **bridge** to the trailer, support **forklift traffic**, and **seal** the transition when paired with shelters and seals.

Decks are typically **high-strength structural steel** (grades such as **S355** are common in European-style literature) with **checkered / tear plate** surfaces for traction. Capacities in many catalogues sit in roughly the **~6–25 t** class for dynamic forklift duty—always size to **heaviest truck + forklift + payload** and **impact**, not static weight alone.

## Operational Mechanisms: Mechanical vs. Hydraulic

* **Mechanical:** Spring / linkage packages without fluid power—lower equipment cost in some lines, higher wear on consumables, operator involvement varies by design.
* **Hydraulic:** **Electro-hydraulic** packs raise the deck and extend the **lip** with push-button operation—smooth motion and programmable interlocks; **velocity fuses** (or equivalent) on hydraulic circuits help arrest uncontrolled descent if support is lost.
* **Air (pneumatic):** Blower-inflated bags on some lines—middle ground on cost; sensitive to debris and maintenance discipline.

## Engineering Safety: Velocity Fuse and Standards

Hydraulic levelers are often designed with **EN 1398**-aligned concepts for mechanical safety. **Velocity fuses** help prevent runaway lowering when flow/velocity spikes—exact implementation is **OEM-specific**. Combine with **vehicle restraint** policy, **communication**, and **lighting** for a full dock safety program.

## Practical Selection Guidelines

* **Dynamic capacity:** Rate for **forklift + max load + impact**; confirm **point load** vs deck thickness.
* **Geometry:** Match **pit length/width/depth** to nominal **deck** and **lip**; confirm **working range** above and below dock for your trailer mix.
* **Power:** Confirm **voltage**, **phase**, and **kW** availability; harmonics and **inrush** may matter on small supplies.
* **Environment:** Choose **finish** and **hydraulic fluid** for **temperature** and **corrosivity** (indoor vs coastal, washdown, etc.).

Request **Jayco’s approved** pit drawings, **capacity chart**, and **electrical** schematic for the exact **nominal size** and **lip** you select.
