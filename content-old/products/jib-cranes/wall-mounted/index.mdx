## The Role of Wall-Mounted JIB Cranes in Workstation Optimization

In manufacturing environments where floor space is at a premium, wall-mounted jib cranes offer a highly efficient, localized material handling solution. They are designed to service specific workstations, assembly lines, or machining centers along the perimeter of a facility, and they reduce reliance on the main overhead EOT crane for light, repetitive lifts.

By handling lighter, frequent lifts locally, they cut the wait time associated with sharing the facility crane, which improves line efficiency where the work point is fixed near a wall or column.

## Structural Architecture and Space Conservation

The defining advantage of a wall-mounted jib crane is conservation of floor space.

* **Minimal floor footprint:** The boom and brackets attach to the facility’s existing structural walls or load-bearing columns instead of a freestanding mast and foundation.
* **Clear aisles:** The area beneath the jib often stays open for forklifts, AGVs, and foot traffic, subject to swing clearance and guarding.
* **Installation profile:** When the building steel is suitable, installation can be faster than pouring a dedicated jib base—though the structural check is non-negotiable.

## Rotation, Span, and Load Path

Wall-mounted jibs trade unlimited slew for a compact attachment to vertical structure.

* **Slew arc:** **180°** is typical when the boom is bracketed along a wall face. Some arrangements allow **up to ~270°** where geometry and stops permit—never assume full 360° without a layout review.
* **Radius:** Industry catalogues often quote **3–10 m** jib reach (effective radius), with many lines advertising **up to ~10 m** on larger brackets—always confirm **tip load** and **building reaction** at your radius.
* **Capacity:** Light kits start around **~250 kg**; many standard lines sit in roughly **0.25–5 t**; **heavy-duty** wall-mounted designs are sometimes offered toward **~10 t** class—**not** every building can support that moment; verify structure and supplier ratings.

## Industry-Typical Operating Parameters (Guide)

The following **industry-style** bands are useful for early sizing only. **Jayco’s approved drawings and load charts** govern any quotation or installation.

* **Lift height:** Driven by headroom and hook path—common marketing ranges often cite roughly **3–15 m** under good ceiling conditions; your slab-to-beam height sets the real limit.
* **Hoisting speed:** Electric hoists are frequently specified in roughly **1–10 m/min** bands for workshop duty; exact values depend on mechanism group and load.
* **Slewing speed:** Small units often use **manual** rotation; larger or busier applications may use **slow powered slew**—match to duty and safety zoning.
* **Mechanism / class:** Workshop jibs are often discussed in **A3–A5**-style classifications in supplier literature; the correct class follows **standards, cycles, and load spectrum** for your site.
* **Power:** Integrated electric hoists commonly use **three-phase** supplies such as **400 V / 50 Hz** in many regions; **440 V** and other voltages appear in project specs—always match the **motor nameplate and local code**.

## Design and Safety Expectations

* **Mounting:** Brackets or plates must be installed on **verified** concrete or steel per **manufacturer and engineer** requirements; bolt grades, embedment, and stiffeners are part of the rated system.
* **Hoist integration:** **Electric chain** and **wire rope** hoists are both common; **pendant** or **radio** control is typical.
* **Protection:** Expect **upper limit** switching on the hoist path, **overload** protection where the hoist provides it, and **clear labeling** of SWL and sweep limits.
* **Visibility:** Equipment is often finished in **high-visibility** yellow or orange (**RAL 1007** is a frequent reference); site-specific paint systems may apply.

## Practical Selection Guidelines

When specifying a wall-mounted jib crane, engineers usually confirm:

* **Structural certification:** The wall or column must be checked for **moment**, **shear**, **pull-out**, and **fatigue** considerations at **rated load** and **worst-case** radius—not just static weight.
* **Hardware:** Through-bolts, clamp brackets, or welded stubs must match **supplier** details; weakening cuts in primary members are generally avoided unless engineered.
* **Hoist and trolley:** Match **class**, **speed**, and **control** to duty; for short spans, manual or push travel may suffice; heavier or faster cycles may need **motorized** motion and interlocks.
